# Strategy Framework Slide Templates

Visual templates and layout descriptions for presenting each strategy framework on a consulting slide. Includes layout structure, axis labels, color conventions, annotations, and action title format.

---

## 1. SWOT 4-Quadrant Layout

**Slide Title (Action Title):** "[Insight]: [Company] has strong [X] but faces [Y] headwinds"

**Layout:** 2x2 grid centered on the slide. Equal-sized quadrants.

| | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | Strengths (top-left, green background #E8F5E9) | Weaknesses (top-right, red background #FFEBEE) |
| **External** | Opportunities (bottom-left, blue background #E3F2FD) | Threats (bottom-right, orange background #FFF3E0) |

**Annotations:**
- Left margin label: "Internal" (top half), "External" (bottom half)
- Top margin label: "Helpful" (left half), "Harmful" (right half)
- 3-5 bullet points per quadrant, each starting with a bold keyword followed by supporting detail
- Use icons or small symbols next to each bullet for visual scanning
- Source footnote at bottom-left

**Action Title Examples:**
- "Strong technology platform and growing market create a clear path to double digital revenue"
- "Talent gaps and rising input costs threaten margin expansion in the near term"

## 2. TOWS Matrix

**Slide Title:** "[Company] has four strategic pathways — SO strategies offer the highest upside"

**Layout:** 4x4 grid. Top row and left column are headers. The four inner quadrants contain strategies.

|  | **Strengths** (list 3-4) | **Weaknesses** (list 3-4) |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunities** (list 3-4) | SO Strategies (green #C8E6C9) | WO Strategies (light blue #B3E5FC) |
| **Threats** (list 3-4) | ST Strategies (yellow #FFF9C4) | WT Strategies (light red #FFCDD2) |

**Annotations:**
- Each strategy cell contains 2-3 numbered strategic options
- Bold the strategy name, then describe in one sentence
- Use arrows connecting specific S/W/O/T items to their corresponding strategy for traceability
- Highlight the recommended strategies with a bold border or star icon

## 3. Ansoff 2x2 Matrix

**Slide Title:** "[Company] should pursue [strategy] as the primary growth vector"

**Layout:** 2x2 grid with clear axis labels.

- **X-axis (columns):** "Existing Products" | "New Products"
- **Y-axis (rows):** "Existing Markets" (top) | "New Markets" (bottom)

| | Existing Products | New Products |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing Markets** | Market Penetration (light green, lowest risk) | Product Development (light yellow, medium risk) |
| **New Markets** | Market Development (light yellow, medium risk) | Diversification (light red, highest risk) |

**Annotations:**
- Each quadrant contains 2-3 specific initiatives with estimated revenue potential
- Use a diagonal arrow from top-left to bottom-right labeled "Increasing Risk"
- Include small risk indicators: one triangle for low risk, two for medium, three for high
- Bubble overlays showing investment required (bubble size) and expected return (color intensity)
- Bottom bar: sequencing timeline showing which quadrant to pursue in Year 1, 2, 3

## 4. BCG Growth-Share Matrix

**Slide Title:** "[X] Stars and [Y] Cash Cows fund the portfolio — [Z] Question Marks require a decision"

**Layout:** Scatter plot on a 2x2 grid.

- **X-axis:** "Relative Market Share" (high on left, low on right — note the reversed axis)
- **Y-axis:** "Market Growth Rate" (low at bottom, high at top)
- **Dividing lines:** Vertical at 1.0x relative share, horizontal at industry average growth rate

**Quadrant Labels and Icons:**
- Top-left: Stars (star icon, gold background)
- Top-right: Question Marks (question mark icon, light purple background)
- Bottom-left: Cash Cows (dollar icon, light green background)
- Bottom-right: Dogs (warning icon, light gray background)

**Annotations:**
- Each business unit is a circle. Circle size = revenue. Circle color = profitability (green = profitable, red = unprofitable).
- Label each circle with the business unit name and revenue figure.
- Use arrows to show projected movement over the next 3 years.
- Include a legend for circle size and color.
- Bottom callout box: recommended resource allocation percentages.

## 5. GE-McKinsey 9-Box Matrix

**Slide Title:** "[X] units should receive investment priority based on attractiveness and strength"

**Layout:** 3x3 grid (9 boxes).

- **X-axis:** "Competitive Strength" — Low | Medium | High (left to right)
- **Y-axis:** "Industry Attractiveness" — Low | Medium | High (bottom to top)

**Color Coding:**
- Top-left 3 boxes (high attractiveness, high strength): Green — Invest/Grow
- Diagonal 3 boxes (medium): Yellow — Hold/Selective Investment
- Bottom-right 3 boxes (low attractiveness, low strength): Red — Harvest/Divest

**Annotations:**
- Each business unit plotted as a circle (size = revenue, position = weighted score).
- Include a scoring table in the appendix showing the weights and scores for each factor.
- Arrows showing recommended strategic direction for each unit.
- Side panel listing the specific factors and weights used for each axis.

## 6. Value Chain Diagram

**Slide Title:** "[Activity X] is the primary source of competitive advantage, contributing [Y]% of margin"

**Layout:** Horizontal arrow shape (chevron) for primary activities along the bottom. Support activities stacked as horizontal bars above.

**Primary Activities (left to right in chevron segments):**
1. Inbound Logistics
2. Operations
3. Outbound Logistics
4. Marketing & Sales
5. Service

**Support Activities (horizontal bars above, spanning the full width):**
1. Firm Infrastructure (top)
2. Human Resource Management
3. Technology Development
4. Procurement (bottom, closest to primary activities)

**Right edge:** "Margin" label with a vertical bar showing profit margin.

**Color Coding:**
- Green segments: activities where the company has a competitive advantage
- Red segments: activities where the company underperforms vs. benchmarks
- Yellow segments: activities at parity with competitors

**Annotations:**
- Cost as a percentage of revenue annotated below each primary activity segment
- Performance rating (1-5 stars or a letter grade) inside each segment
- Callout boxes pointing to 2-3 key insights: "Operations cost is 15% above benchmark" or "Service quality drives 40% of customer retention"
- Benchmark comparison bar at the bottom showing competitor cost structure

## 7. Business Model Canvas 9-Block

**Slide Title:** "[Company]'s model is [characterization] — the key vulnerability is [block]"

**Layout:** Standard Osterwalder layout — a single page divided into 9 blocks:

```
+-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
| Key Partners      | Key Activities    | Value             | Customer          | Customer          |
|                   |                   | Propositions      | Relationships     | Segments          |
|                   +-------------------+ (center,          +-------------------+                   |
|                   | Key Resources     |  tallest block)   | Channels          |                   |
+-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
| Cost Structure (spans left half)      | Revenue Streams (spans right half)                      |
+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Color Coding:**
- Left side (Partners, Activities, Resources, Cost): operational engine — use blue tones
- Center (Value Proposition): the core — use bold accent color (gold or dark blue)
- Right side (Relationships, Channels, Segments, Revenue): customer-facing — use green tones

**Annotations:**
- 3-5 concise bullet points per block
- Bold the most critical item in each block
- Use connecting lines or arrows between blocks to show key dependencies
- Highlight risks with a red exclamation mark icon

## 8. Three Horizons Timeline

**Slide Title:** "[Company] must balance core optimization with [adjacent/transformational] investment"

**Layout:** X-axis = time (0-10 years). Y-axis = value/profit contribution. Three overlapping S-curves.

- **Horizon 1 (blue):** Starts high on the left, peaks in Year 1-2, then gradually declines. Label: "Core Business — Optimize & Defend"
- **Horizon 2 (green):** Starts low, rises through Year 2-5, peaks around Year 5. Label: "Adjacent Growth — Extend & Build"
- **Horizon 3 (orange):** Starts near zero, begins rising around Year 3-5, grows steeply through Year 10. Label: "Transformational — Create & Explore"

**Annotations:**
- Resource allocation callout: "70% / 20% / 10%" positioned near the left side
- Specific initiatives listed under each horizon in a table below the chart
- Key milestones marked on the timeline (e.g., "Launch MVP in adjacent market — Q3 Year 2")
- Vertical "today" line marking the current position

## 9. Porter's Five Forces Diamond

**Slide Title:** "[Force X] is the dominant force constraining industry profitability"

**Layout:** Diamond/hub-and-spoke arrangement.

- **Center box:** "Competitive Rivalry" (largest box)
- **Top:** "Threat of New Entrants" with downward arrow to center
- **Bottom:** "Threat of Substitutes" with upward arrow to center
- **Left:** "Supplier Power" with rightward arrow to center
- **Right:** "Buyer Power" with leftward arrow to center

**Color Coding:**
- Green border: Low force (favorable for industry profitability)
- Yellow border: Medium force
- Red border: High force (unfavorable)

**Annotations:**
- Inside each box: 2-3 key factors driving the force rating
- Force rating displayed prominently: "HIGH," "MEDIUM," or "LOW"
- Trend arrows (up/down/flat) showing how each force is expected to change over 3-5 years
- Bottom summary bar: "Overall Industry Attractiveness: [High/Medium/Low]"

## 10. Blue Ocean Value Curve

**Slide Title:** "[Company] creates a new value curve by [eliminating X] and [creating Y]"

**Layout:** Line chart.

- **X-axis:** Key competing factors (6-10 factors that define industry competition)
- **Y-axis:** Offering level (Low to High)

**Lines:**
- Industry average or key competitor(s): gray or blue dashed lines
- Company's proposed new value curve: bold red or orange solid line

**Annotations:**
- Four Actions Framework callout boxes positioned around the chart:
  - "Eliminate" (red X icon) pointing to factors reduced to zero
  - "Reduce" (down arrow) pointing to factors significantly lowered
  - "Raise" (up arrow) pointing to factors significantly increased
  - "Create" (star icon) pointing to new factors added
- Shaded area between the new curve and competitor curves to highlight divergence
- Bottom table listing each factor with the specific action and rationale

## 11. Balanced Scorecard 4-Perspective

**Slide Title:** "[Company]'s scorecard reveals strong financial results but lagging [perspective]"

**Layout:** Four boxes arranged in a diamond or 2x2 pattern, with connecting arrows showing cause-and-effect linkages.

- **Top:** Financial Perspective (green header)
- **Left:** Customer Perspective (blue header)
- **Right:** Internal Process Perspective (orange header)
- **Bottom:** Learning & Growth Perspective (purple header)
- **Center:** Vision & Strategy (connecting all four)

**Each perspective box contains a mini-table:**

| Objective | Measure | Target | Actual | Status |
|-----------|---------|--------|--------|--------|
| Grow revenue | Revenue growth % | 15% | 12% | Yellow |

**Color Coding for Status:**
- Green: on track (actual >= 90% of target)
- Yellow: at risk (actual between 70-89% of target)
- Red: off track (actual < 70% of target)

**Annotations:**
- Arrows between perspectives showing causal logic (e.g., "Employee training → Process efficiency → Customer satisfaction → Revenue growth")
- Summary callout: number of green/yellow/red measures across all perspectives
- Trend indicators (up/down/flat arrows) next to each measure
