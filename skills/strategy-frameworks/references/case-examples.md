# Strategy Framework Case Examples

Five mini case studies demonstrating framework application in realistic consulting scenarios. Each case presents the situation, the frameworks applied, key findings, and the resulting recommendation.

---

## Case 1: Market Entry — MedSync Software Entering the Healthcare Vertical

### Situation
MedSync is a $150M mid-size enterprise software company specializing in workflow automation for financial services. With growth slowing in its core market (8% annually, down from 15% three years ago), the CEO is considering entering the healthcare vertical, where demand for workflow automation is growing at 22% annually. MedSync has no healthcare clients, no regulatory expertise (HIPAA, FDA), and no relationships with hospital IT departments. However, the company's core platform is technically adaptable and its engineering team is strong. A competitor recently raised $80M to pursue the same opportunity.

### Frameworks Applied: Ansoff Matrix + Porter's Five Forces

**Ansoff Matrix Positioning:** This is a Market Development play — taking an existing product (workflow automation) to a new market (healthcare). This sits at medium risk on the Ansoff spectrum, with a benchmark success rate of approximately 50%.

**Porter's Five Forces Assessment of Healthcare IT:**
- **Competitive Rivalry:** High. Several entrenched players (Epic, Cerner) dominate, plus a wave of well-funded startups. Switching costs for hospitals are extremely high.
- **Buyer Power:** High. Hospital systems are sophisticated buyers with dedicated procurement teams, and they consolidate purchasing to extract volume discounts.
- **Supplier Power:** Low. Cloud infrastructure and engineering talent are available, though healthcare-specialized talent is scarce.
- **Threat of New Entrants:** Medium. Regulatory requirements (HIPAA certification, FDA compliance) create significant barriers, but venture capital is actively funding new entrants.
- **Threat of Substitutes:** Low. Manual processes are the primary alternative, and the regulatory push for digitization makes reversion unlikely.

### Key Findings
The industry is attractive in terms of growth but structurally challenging due to high rivalry and buyer power. MedSync's lack of domain expertise and regulatory credentials is a significant gap. The 18-24 month timeline to achieve HIPAA certification and build reference customers creates a window of vulnerability where capital is being spent without revenue.

### Recommendation
Pursue healthcare entry through a partnership or acquisition rather than organic build. Acquire a small, HIPAA-certified healthcare workflow company ($10-25M range) to gain instant regulatory credentials, domain expertise, and reference customers. This converts a risky Market Development play into a lower-risk capability acquisition. Budget $30-40M including integration costs, with a break-even target of Year 3.

## Case 2: Turnaround — Bridgeport Retail Chain Repositioning

### Situation
Bridgeport Home Goods is a 120-store regional home furnishings retailer with $480M in annual revenue. Over the past three years, EBITDA margin has declined from 12% to 4%, driven by a combination of rising rent costs, an undifferentiated product assortment competing directly with Amazon and Wayfair on price, a bloated corporate headquarters (overhead grew 35% while revenue was flat), and a supply chain built for bulk imports from China that is increasingly costly due to tariffs. The board has given the CEO 18 months to demonstrate a credible turnaround plan or the company will be sold.

### Frameworks Applied: Value Chain Analysis + SWOT

**Value Chain Analysis Findings:**
- **Inbound Logistics (18% of COGS):** 40% above benchmark due to reliance on a single import corridor. No nearshoring or multi-source strategy.
- **Operations/Store Operations (22% of revenue):** In line with peers, but 30 of 120 stores are in declining malls with foot traffic down 40% in five years.
- **Marketing & Sales (8% of revenue):** 60% of the marketing budget is allocated to traditional channels (print, radio) with no measurable ROI. Digital marketing is underdeveloped.
- **Corporate Overhead (11% of revenue):** 300 basis points above the industry median of 8%, driven by headquarters expansion during the growth years.

**SWOT Analysis:**
- **Strengths:** Strong brand recognition in the Mid-Atlantic region, loyal customer base (65% repeat purchase rate), experienced store managers.
- **Weaknesses:** No e-commerce capability, undifferentiated product assortment, high fixed costs, outdated technology infrastructure.
- **Opportunities:** Home furnishing market is growing at 6% annually, consumers are shifting toward curated/local brands, remote work driving home office spending.
- **Threats:** Amazon and Wayfair dominate online, tariff costs rising, commercial real estate flexibility limited by long-term leases.

### Key Findings
The value chain analysis reveals $55-65M in addressable cost reduction. The largest opportunities are in supply chain diversification ($15-20M), corporate overhead reduction ($12-15M), closure of the 30 underperforming stores ($10-15M in lease savings over time), and marketing reallocation from traditional to digital channels ($8-10M in efficiency gains).

### Recommendation
Execute a two-phase turnaround. Phase 1 (Months 1-6): Cut corporate overhead by 30%, close or renegotiate leases on the bottom 25 stores, diversify supply chain with nearshore suppliers in Mexico and Vietnam. Phase 2 (Months 6-18): Launch e-commerce platform, reposition brand as a curated home goods retailer (differentiation strategy vs. competing on price), and reallocate 70% of marketing budget to digital channels. Target: restore EBITDA margin to 9% within 18 months.

## Case 3: Digital Disruption — Heritage National Bank vs. Fintech

### Situation
Heritage National Bank is a $12B-asset community bank with 85 branches across three states. It has served small businesses and retail customers for 90 years. Over the past five years, Heritage has lost 15% of its small business checking accounts to fintech platforms offering faster onboarding, lower fees, integrated invoicing, and real-time payments. The bank's mobile app has a 2.8-star rating. Its core banking system is 20 years old. However, Heritage maintains $8B in low-cost deposits, deep community relationships, and a trusted brand, advantages that fintechs struggle to replicate. The board is debating whether to build digital capabilities in-house, partner with fintechs, or accept managed decline.

### Frameworks Applied: Blue Ocean Strategy + Three Horizons

**Blue Ocean Strategy Canvas:**
Mapped Heritage against two leading fintech competitors across ten competing factors. Heritage outperforms on trust/brand, relationship depth, local presence, full-service offerings, and regulatory stability. Fintechs outperform on onboarding speed, fee transparency, mobile experience, integration with business tools, and data-driven insights.

**Four Actions Framework:**
- **Eliminate:** Branch-based account opening process (move fully digital).
- **Reduce:** Fee complexity (simplify from 47 fee types to 8 transparent tiers).
- **Raise:** Mobile experience quality (target 4.5-star rating), relationship manager accessibility (video banking).
- **Create:** "Community Business Network" — a platform connecting Heritage's small business customers to each other for referrals, procurement, and local partnerships. No fintech offers this.

**Three Horizons Application:**
- **Horizon 1 (0-2 years):** Modernize the core — replace the core banking system, rebuild the mobile app, digitize account opening. Consolidate 85 branches to 60.
- **Horizon 2 (2-5 years):** Launch the Community Business Network platform, introduce embedded lending at point of sale for small business customers, and deploy AI-driven financial insights.
- **Horizon 3 (5-10 years):** Evolve into a community commerce platform that goes beyond banking — facilitating local economic ecosystems with Heritage as the trusted infrastructure layer.

### Key Findings
Heritage does not need to beat fintechs at their own game. The blue ocean analysis reveals that trust, relationships, and local community connectedness are dimensions that fintechs cannot easily replicate. The winning strategy is to combine Heritage's relational strengths with fintech-grade digital capabilities, creating a value curve that is distinct from both traditional banks and fintechs.

### Recommendation
Invest $45M over three years in core modernization (Horizon 1) while simultaneously developing the Community Business Network concept (Horizon 2). Partner with a banking-as-a-service provider for the core system replacement to accelerate Horizon 1 from 3 years to 18 months. Protect the deposit base and community relationships while upgrading the digital experience. Target: zero net account losses by Year 2, 10% net account growth by Year 4.

## Case 4: M&A Evaluation — Granite Peak Capital Assessing a SaaS Acquisition

### Situation
Granite Peak Capital, a mid-market private equity firm, is evaluating the acquisition of CloudPulse, a $35M ARR B2B SaaS platform providing real-time supply chain visibility to mid-market manufacturers. CloudPulse is growing at 40% annually with 92% gross margins, 115% net dollar retention, and a CAC payback period of 14 months. The asking price is $280M (8x ARR). Granite Peak needs to determine whether CloudPulse has a defensible competitive position and a scalable business model to justify the premium valuation.

### Frameworks Applied: VRIO Analysis + Business Model Canvas

**VRIO Analysis (Assessing Competitive Advantage):**
- **Proprietary Data Network (V-R-I-O):** CloudPulse has 2,200 connected supply chain nodes generating proprietary data. Valuable: yes, enables real-time visibility. Rare: yes, largest independent network in the mid-market segment. Costly to Imitate: yes, 4+ year data accumulation advantage. Organized to Exploit: yes, dedicated data science team of 15. **Verdict: Sustained competitive advantage.**
- **Machine Learning Models (V-R-I):** Predictive disruption models trained on proprietary data. Valuable, rare, and somewhat costly to imitate, but the company has not fully organized to exploit them commercially. **Verdict: Underexploited competitive advantage — upside opportunity.**
- **Customer Success Methodology (V-R):** High-touch onboarding drives the 115% net retention. Valuable and somewhat rare, but replicable by well-funded competitors. **Verdict: Temporary competitive advantage — not a moat.**

**Business Model Canvas Assessment:**
- **Strengths:** Strong value proposition (quantifiable ROI of 3-5x for customers), efficient land-and-expand revenue model, platform network effects (each new node increases value for all participants).
- **Vulnerabilities:** Customer concentration (top 10 customers = 35% of ARR), single-channel go-to-market (direct sales only, no partnerships or marketplace), and Key Person risk (CTO and three senior engineers hold critical platform knowledge).

### Key Findings
CloudPulse's proprietary data network is a genuine and durable competitive advantage that justifies premium valuation. The ML models represent an underexploited asset with significant upside. However, customer concentration, single-channel distribution, and key person risk are real vulnerabilities that need to be addressed post-acquisition.

### Recommendation
Proceed with the acquisition at $260-270M (7.5x ARR) with a structured earnout to $290M contingent on retention of the CTO and key engineers for 3 years. Post-acquisition plan: (1) diversify customer base by targeting the long tail of mid-market manufacturers, (2) launch a channel partner program to reduce CAC and diversify distribution, (3) commercialize the ML predictive models as a premium tier, and (4) implement key person risk mitigation through documentation, cross-training, and equity retention packages. Target: $80M ARR by Year 3, positioning for a $600M+ exit.

## Case 5: Cost Optimization — Precision Manufacturing Group Margin Recovery

### Situation
Precision Manufacturing Group (PMG) is a $620M industrial components manufacturer supplying the automotive and aerospace sectors. Gross margins have declined from 32% to 24% over four years due to raw material cost increases (steel and aluminum up 45%), energy cost spikes, rising labor costs in its domestic facilities, and competitive price pressure from Asian manufacturers. The CEO has set a target of restoring gross margins to 28% within two years without sacrificing quality, which is PMG's primary competitive differentiator in the aerospace segment.

### Frameworks Applied: Value Chain Analysis + Benchmarking

**Value Chain Cost Analysis (% of COGS):**
- **Raw Materials / Procurement (42%):** PMG buys from 6 primary steel and aluminum suppliers with annual contracts. Benchmarking reveals PMG pays 8-12% above market median due to fragmented purchasing across three divisions, no hedging program, and no recycling/reclamation of scrap material (industry leaders reclaim 15-20% of input materials).
- **Manufacturing Operations (28%):** PMG operates 4 domestic plants at an average 71% utilization. Industry benchmark is 85%. Two plants are subscale and underperforming. Energy costs are 18% above benchmark due to outdated equipment with poor energy efficiency.
- **Quality Control & Testing (12%):** Above benchmark (industry average is 8%), but this is intentional — aerospace customers require extensive testing and certification. This is a source of differentiation, not a cost to cut.
- **Logistics & Distribution (10%):** In line with peers. Minor optimization opportunities in route planning and warehouse consolidation.
- **Overhead & SGA (8%):** Slightly above benchmark (6.5%) due to duplicative functions across divisions.

**Benchmarking Results (vs. Top Quartile Peers):**
- Procurement: $18-24M annual savings opportunity through consolidated purchasing, commodity hedging, and scrap reclamation
- Manufacturing: $12-16M savings from consolidating four plants to three (close the smallest, shift volume to the two largest) and investing $8M in energy-efficient equipment
- Overhead: $5-8M savings from shared services consolidation across divisions

### Key Findings
The total addressable cost improvement is $35-48M, which would improve gross margins by 5.6-7.7 percentage points, exceeding the 4-point improvement target. Critically, the quality control function should not be cut despite being above benchmark. It is the foundation of PMG's pricing power in aerospace, where it commands a 15-20% price premium over competitors.

### Recommendation
Execute a 24-month margin recovery program in three waves. Wave 1 (Months 1-6): Consolidate procurement across divisions, implement commodity hedging for steel and aluminum, and launch a scrap reclamation program. Expected savings: $15-20M. Wave 2 (Months 6-14): Consolidate from four plants to three, invest $8M in energy-efficient equipment for the two anchor plants. Expected savings: $10-14M. Wave 3 (Months 12-24): Implement shared services for finance, HR, and IT across divisions. Expected savings: $5-8M. Protect the quality control function and use the restored margins to reinvest in aerospace certification capabilities, deepening the competitive moat. Target: 28.5% gross margin by end of Year 2.
