# Stakeholder Mapping Guide

## Power/Interest Grid

Map every stakeholder on a 2×2 matrix:

```
              HIGH INTEREST
                   |
    Keep Satisfied |  Manage Closely
    (High power,   |  (High power,
     low interest)  |   high interest)
                   |
  ─────────────────┼─────────────────
                   |
    Monitor        |  Keep Informed
    (Low power,    |  (Low power,
     low interest)  |   high interest)
                   |
              LOW INTEREST
   LOW POWER ─────────── HIGH POWER
```

### Quadrant Strategies

| Quadrant | Strategy | Engagement Level | Actions |
|----------|----------|-----------------|---------|
| **Manage Closely** | Active partnership | Weekly 1:1s, co-design | Involve in decisions, seek input early, make them champions |
| **Keep Satisfied** | Proactive updates | Bi-weekly briefings | Address concerns quickly, don't overwhelm with detail, respect their time |
| **Keep Informed** | Regular communication | Monthly updates, town halls | Provide information, listen to concerns, leverage as advocates |
| **Monitor** | Minimal engagement | Quarterly updates | Watch for movement to other quadrants, standard communications |

### Stance Definitions

| Stance | Description | Behavioral Indicators |
|--------|------------|----------------------|
| **Champion** | Actively promotes the change, influences others | Volunteers for pilot, speaks positively, recruits others |
| **Supporter** | Agrees with the change, will participate | Attends meetings, follows through, asks constructive questions |
| **Neutral** | Neither supports nor opposes, undecided | Waits to see, follows the majority, non-committal |
| **Resistant** | Disagrees, may slow progress | Raises objections, misses deadlines, negative body language |
| **Blocker** | Actively works against the change | Refuses to participate, undermines in meetings, lobbies against |

## Influence Strategies by Stakeholder Type

### Moving Stakeholders to Champion

| From | To | Strategy | Tactics |
|------|----|----------|---------|
| **Blocker → Resistant** | Reduce active opposition | Listen first, acknowledge legitimate concerns, find common ground | 1:1 meetings, private conversations, address fears directly |
| **Resistant → Neutral** | Remove barriers to acceptance | Show personal benefit ("WIIFM"), provide evidence, offer support | Data presentations, peer testimonials, pilot participation |
| **Neutral → Supporter** | Build positive engagement | Involve in design, give ownership of a piece | Working group membership, early wins visibility, recognition |
| **Supporter → Champion** | Activate advocacy | Empower to influence others, celebrate their contributions | Public recognition, leadership role in change, access to executives |

### Influence Tactics Menu

| Tactic | When to Use | Effort | Effectiveness |
|--------|------------|--------|--------------|
| **Personal appeal** | Close relationship exists | Low | High |
| **Rational persuasion** | Analytical stakeholders | Medium | Medium-High |
| **Inspirational appeal** | Values-driven stakeholders | Low | High |
| **Consultation** | Stakeholder wants input | Medium | High |
| **Coalition building** | Need to shift group dynamics | High | Very High |
| **Exchange** | Transactional relationship | Medium | Medium |
| **Legitimating** | Authority/policy-driven | Low | Medium |
| **Pressure** | Last resort, urgent timeline | Low | Low (short-term only) |

## Coalition Building Strategy

### The Coalition Math

For major organizational change, you typically need:
- **15–20%** active champions to reach tipping point
- **30–40%** supporters to create momentum
- **Maximum 10%** active blockers (above this, the change is at risk)

### Building the Coalition — 5 Steps

1. **Identify your natural allies** — Who already agrees? Start here for quick wins
2. **Recruit influential neutrals** — Target high-power neutrals first; they swing the majority
3. **Isolate blockers** — Don't waste energy converting committed blockers; route around them
4. **Create a "guiding coalition"** — 5–8 diverse leaders who collectively represent the organization
5. **Equip champions with tools** — Talking points, data, success stories they can share

### Guiding Coalition Composition

The ideal coalition includes:
- 1 executive sponsor (positional power)
- 2–3 respected middle managers (operational influence)
- 1–2 informal influencers (social influence)
- 1 subject matter expert (credibility)
- 1 skeptic-turned-supporter (authenticity)

## Worked Example 1: ERP Implementation at Manufacturing Company

**Context:** $500M revenue manufacturer replacing 15-year-old ERP with SAP S/4HANA. 2,000 employees affected, 18-month timeline.

### Stakeholder Map

| Stakeholder | Power | Interest | Stance | Strategy |
|-------------|:-----:|:--------:|:------:|----------|
| CEO | 5 | 4 | Champion | Keep as visible sponsor; monthly steering updates |
| CFO | 5 | 5 | Champion | Co-design financial modules; leverage as peer influencer |
| VP Manufacturing | 5 | 5 | Resistant | High priority! 1:1 sessions to address production concerns; pilot in his plant first |
| VP Sales | 4 | 3 | Neutral | Show CRM integration benefits; include in design of sales modules |
| IT Director | 4 | 5 | Champion | Technical lead; give authority over architecture decisions |
| Plant Managers (6) | 3 | 5 | Mixed | Involve in requirements; address downtime fears with phased rollout plan |
| Finance Team (25) | 2 | 5 | Supportive | Heavy training; they'll be super-users; recognize their expertise |
| Shop Floor Workers (800) | 1 | 4 | Resistant | Simple messaging; hands-on training; shop floor champions program |
| Union Rep | 3 | 4 | Resistant | Early engagement; address job security fears; involve in training design |
| External Customers | 2 | 2 | Monitor | Prepare for service disruptions during cutover; proactive communication |

**Key Insight:** The VP Manufacturing is the swing stakeholder — high power, high interest, currently resistant. Converting him to supporter is the #1 priority. Strategy: involve him in vendor selection, pilot in his plant, assign a dedicated change liaison.

## Worked Example 2: Organizational Restructure

**Context:** Professional services firm (1,200 employees) restructuring from geography-based to industry-based practice groups. Affects all managing directors and senior managers.

### Stakeholder Map

| Stakeholder | Power | Interest | Stance | Strategy |
|-------------|:-----:|:--------:|:------:|----------|
| Managing Partner | 5 | 5 | Champion | Visible leadership; weekly town halls; "why" messaging |
| Practice Leaders (new role, 5) | 4 | 5 | Supportive | They gain authority; involve in design of new structure |
| Regional MDs (losing role, 4) | 4 | 5 | Resistant/Blocker | Critical! Redesign roles to preserve status; 1:1 transition plans |
| Senior Managers (80) | 3 | 5 | Mixed | Many will change reporting lines; clear communication on career paths |
| HR Director | 3 | 4 | Supportive | Partner on talent implications; lead compensation redesign |
| Key Clients (top 20) | 3 | 3 | Neutral | Relationship manager continuity assurance; benefits messaging |
| Junior staff (900) | 1 | 3 | Neutral | Minimal direct impact; standard communications; reassure on jobs |
| Board | 5 | 3 | Supportive | Quarterly updates; milestone reporting; financial impact tracking |

**Key Insight:** The 4 Regional MDs who lose their roles are the highest-risk stakeholders. Two options: (a) transition them to Practice Leader roles (if capable), or (b) create senior client relationship roles that preserve status and compensation. One-on-one career conversations within 48 hours of announcement are critical.
