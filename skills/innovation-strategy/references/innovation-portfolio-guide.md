# Innovation Portfolio Guide

## Portfolio Allocation Framework

### The 70/20/10 Model

| Category | Allocation | Definition | Expected Returns | Time Horizon | Risk Profile |
|----------|:---------:|-----------|:----------------:|:------------:|:------------:|
| **Core** | ~70% | Improvements to existing products/services for existing customers | 10–20% incremental improvement | 0–12 months | Low |
| **Adjacent** | ~20% | New offerings for existing customers, or existing offerings for new markets | New revenue stream, moderate growth | 12–36 months | Medium |
| **Transformational** | ~10% | New products for new markets, new business models | Breakthrough, 10x potential | 36+ months | High |

**Key insight:** Companies that allocate roughly 70/20/10 generate a disproportionate return from their 10% transformational bet — research shows ~70% of returns come from core, but the transformational 10% typically generates ~20% of long-term value creation.

### Three Horizons Mapping

| Horizon | Focus | Examples | Success Metrics | Management Approach |
|:-------:|-------|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| **H1** | Extend and defend current business | Feature improvements, cost reduction, market share gain | Revenue growth, margin improvement, NPS | Operational management, budgets, KPIs |
| **H2** | Build emerging businesses | New product lines, new customer segments, geographic expansion | New revenue, customer acquisition, market share in new segment | Incubation, dedicated team, milestones |
| **H3** | Create viable options | R&D, pilots, experiments, venture investments | Learning velocity, assumptions validated, option value | Venture-style, high autonomy, experiment-driven |

### Diagnosing Portfolio Imbalance

| Symptom | Likely Problem | Prescription |
|---------|---------------|-------------|
| All projects are core/incremental | **Under-investing in the future** — current revenue is good but no pipeline for disruption | Ring-fence 20–30% for H2/H3; create innovation fund with separate governance |
| All projects are transformational | **Ignoring the core business** — exciting ideas but core is eroding | Redirect resources to defend current revenue; fix before you fly |
| Heavy H2, weak H1 and H3 | **Stuck in the middle** — not defending core or creating breakthroughs | Sharpen H1 focus on margin improvement; move some H2 bets to H3 |
| Revenue declining, no H2/H3 pipeline | **Innovation debt** — years of underinvestment | Emergency innovation sprint; consider M&A for capability catch-up |

## Innovation Scoring Models

### Initiative Scoring Template

Score each initiative 1–5 on these criteria:

| Criteria | Weight | 1 (Low) | 3 (Medium) | 5 (High) |
|----------|:------:|---------|-----------|----------|
| **Strategic alignment** | 20% | Tangential to strategy | Aligned | Core to strategic priorities |
| **Market attractiveness** | 15% | Small/declining market | Moderate market | Large, growing market |
| **Customer desirability** | 15% | Unvalidated assumption | Some evidence | Strong evidence of demand |
| **Technical feasibility** | 15% | Major unknowns | Achievable with stretch | Proven technology |
| **Business viability** | 15% | Path to profitability unclear | Viable with assumptions | Clear, attractive unit economics |
| **Competitive advantage** | 10% | Easy to copy | Some defensibility | Strong moat (IP, network, data) |
| **Team readiness** | 10% | No team, skills gap | Team identified, some gaps | Strong team in place |

## Resource Allocation Across Portfolio

### Allocation by Resource Type

| Resource | Core (70%) | Adjacent (20%) | Transformational (10%) |
|----------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Budget** | Operating budget, incremental | Dedicated budget, ring-fenced | Venture fund, separate P&L |
| **People** | Existing teams, part-time | Dedicated team, cross-functional | Autonomous team, full-time |
| **Leadership attention** | BAU management, monthly reviews | Executive sponsor, bi-weekly | CEO/Board visibility, weekly |
| **Risk tolerance** | Low — must hit targets | Medium — some pivots expected | High — most will fail |
| **Decision speed** | Standard governance | Accelerated approvals | Autonomous within guardrails |

### Innovation Budgeting Approaches

| Approach | How It Works | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|----------|-------------|------|------|---------|
| **Fixed % of revenue** | Allocate X% annually | Predictable, scales with business | May not match opportunity size | Established companies |
| **Separate P&L** | Innovation unit with own budget | Protected from cost-cutting, accountability | Can become isolated | Large companies |
| **Venture-style** | Stage-gated funding, milestone-based | Disciplined, kills losers fast | Can be bureaucratic | Portfolio with many bets |
| **Competition-based** | Match or exceed competitor R&D spend | Ensures competitiveness | Reactive, not strategic | Competitive markets |
| **Outcome-based** | Fund based on validated learning | Most efficient use of capital | Harder to plan, requires maturity | Mature innovation functions |

## Portfolio Governance

### Review Cadence

| Review | Frequency | Attendees | Focus | Decisions |
|--------|:---------:|----------|-------|----------|
| **Innovation Board** | Monthly | CEO, CPO, CTO, CFO, innovation lead | Portfolio performance, resource allocation, strategic alignment | Fund/kill/pivot decisions |
| **Stage-gate reviews** | Per milestone | Sponsor, PM, innovation lead | Individual initiative progress, gate criteria | Go/no-go for next stage |
| **Portfolio rebalancing** | Quarterly | Innovation Board | Overall allocation, new opportunities, portfolio health | Budget reallocation, new bets, sunset decisions |
| **Strategy alignment** | Annual | Board + leadership | Innovation strategy vs. corporate strategy | Multi-year innovation roadmap, budget envelope |

### Governance Principles

1. **Separate governance for H1 vs. H2/H3** — Don't judge experiments by operational KPIs
2. **Kill fast, not slow** — Better to kill 10 bad ideas quickly than let 1 bad idea linger
3. **Protect the budget** — Innovation funds should not be raided for operational shortfalls
4. **Celebrate learning, not just success** — A well-run experiment that disproves a hypothesis is valuable
5. **Diversity of bets** — No single bet should be >30% of innovation portfolio

## Worked Example: $10M Innovation Budget

**Context:** $500M revenue industrial company allocating $10M (2% of revenue) to innovation.

### Portfolio Allocation

| Category | Budget | # Initiatives | Avg Investment | Examples |
|----------|:------:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Core (70%)** | $7M | 10–15 | $500K | Process automation, product quality improvement, customer portal upgrade, supply chain optimization |
| **Adjacent (20%)** | $2M | 3–5 | $500K | IoT-enabled product line, aftermarket services platform, international market pilot |
| **Transformational (10%)** | $1M | 2–3 | $350K | AI predictive maintenance SaaS, circular economy pilot, digital twin R&D |

### Initiative Scoring

| Initiative | Strategic | Market | Customer | Feasibility | Viability | Advantage | Team | **Score** | Category | Budget |
|-----------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Factory automation Phase 2 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 5 | **4.4** | Core | $1.5M |
| Customer self-service portal | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | **4.2** | Core | $800K |
| IoT sensor product line | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | **3.9** | Adjacent | $700K |
| Aftermarket services platform | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | **3.7** | Adjacent | $600K |
| AI predictive maintenance SaaS | 5 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 5 | 2 | **3.3** | Transformational | $500K |
| Digital twin R&D | 4 | 4 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 4 | 2 | **2.7** | Transformational | $300K |

### Expected Returns

| Category | Investment | Expected Return (3-year) | Expected ROI | Risk-Adjusted Return |
|----------|:---------:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Core | $7M | $15M (cost savings + revenue improvement) | 114% | $12M (80% confidence) |
| Adjacent | $2M | $8M (new revenue streams) | 300% | $3.2M (40% confidence) |
| Transformational | $1M | $20M (option value if 1 succeeds) | 1,900% | $2M (10% confidence) |
| **Total** | **$10M** | **$43M** | **330%** | **$17.2M** |

**Key insight:** The transformational bets have the highest potential return but also highest risk. Only 1 in 3 transformational bets is expected to succeed — but the one that does could generate 20x return.
