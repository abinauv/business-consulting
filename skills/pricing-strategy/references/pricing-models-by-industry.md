# Pricing Models by Industry

Comprehensive reference for pricing models, benchmarks, and norms across major industries.

---

## SaaS / Software

### Pricing Models

**Per-Seat (Per-User) Pricing**
- Charge per named user or active user per month/year
- Examples: Slack, Salesforce, Jira
- Best for: collaboration tools, productivity software, tools where value scales with headcount
- Pros: predictable revenue, scales with customer growth, easy to understand
- Cons: discourages broad adoption, incentivizes seat sharing, penalizes large teams

**Typical benchmarks:**
- SMB: $5-$30/user/month
- Mid-market: $20-$100/user/month
- Enterprise: $50-$300/user/month (often custom)

**Usage-Based Pricing**
- Charge based on consumption: API calls, data processed, transactions, compute hours
- Examples: AWS, Twilio, Stripe, Snowflake
- Best for: infrastructure, APIs, data platforms, tools with variable consumption
- Pros: low entry barrier, aligns cost with value, natural expansion revenue
- Cons: revenue volatility, hard for customer to predict costs, forecast complexity

**Typical benchmarks:**
- API calls: $0.001-$0.05 per call (varies massively by complexity)
- Data storage: $0.01-$0.10 per GB/month
- Transaction fees: 1-3% of transaction value
- Compute: $0.01-$0.50 per compute-hour

**Tiered Flat-Rate Pricing**
- Fixed monthly/annual fee per tier with defined feature sets and limits
- Examples: Mailchimp, HubSpot, Notion
- Best for: SMB-focused tools, products with clear feature tiers
- Pros: simple, predictable for customer, easy to communicate
- Cons: may leave money on the table with high-usage customers, cliff effects at tier boundaries

**Typical benchmarks:**
- Entry tier: $0-$29/month
- Mid tier: $49-$199/month
- Premium tier: $199-$999/month
- Enterprise: custom ($500-$10,000+/month)

**Freemium / Product-Led Growth**
- Free tier with limited features or usage; paid tiers for more
- Examples: Zoom, Dropbox, Canva, Figma
- Best for: wide TAM, strong network effects, viral potential, low marginal cost
- Pros: massive top-of-funnel, product-market fit signal, viral growth
- Cons: high cost of free users, low conversion rates, free users may never pay

**Benchmarks:**
- Free-to-paid conversion: 2-5% (typical), 5-8% (good), 8-12% (excellent)
- Time to convert: median 14-30 days for quick converters; 60-180 days for full base
- Free user cost: $0.50-$5.00/month per free user (hosting, support)

**Hybrid Pricing (Seat + Usage)**
- Base per-seat fee plus usage-based component
- Examples: Datadog (host-based + usage), HubSpot (seat + contacts)
- Best for: platforms with both user-level and consumption-level value
- Pros: predictable base revenue with usage upside, captures both dimensions of value
- Cons: complex to communicate, harder for customer to budget

### SaaS Industry Norms
- Gross margin: 70-85%
- Annual price escalator: 3-7% (often tied to CPI or flat)
- Monthly vs. annual discount: 15-20% for annual commitment
- Average contract value (ACV) by segment:
  - SMB: $1,000-$10,000/year
  - Mid-market: $10,000-$100,000/year
  - Enterprise: $100,000-$1,000,000+/year
- Net revenue retention best-in-class: 120-140%
- Expansion revenue as % of new ARR: 20-40% (mature companies)

## Professional Services

### Pricing Models

**Hourly / Time & Materials**
- Bill per hour worked, plus expenses
- Best for: unpredictable scope, advisory, legal, accounting
- Pros: simple, flexible, fair for variable-scope work
- Cons: misaligns incentives (rewards slow work), client budget anxiety, commoditizes expertise

**Typical rates:**
| Role Level | Consulting | Legal | Accounting | IT Services |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Junior / Analyst | $100-$200/hr | $150-$300/hr | $100-$200/hr | $75-$150/hr |
| Manager / Senior | $200-$400/hr | $300-$600/hr | $200-$400/hr | $150-$250/hr |
| Partner / Principal | $400-$800/hr | $500-$1,500/hr | $350-$700/hr | $200-$400/hr |
| Specialist / Expert | $500-$1,200/hr | $600-$2,000/hr | $400-$900/hr | $250-$500/hr |

**Project-Based / Fixed Fee**
- Single price for a defined deliverable or scope of work
- Best for: well-defined projects, strategy engagements, implementation projects
- Pros: predictable for client, rewards efficiency, outcome-oriented
- Cons: scope creep risk, requires accurate estimation, change order friction

**Typical benchmarks:**
- Strategy project (4-8 weeks): $50,000-$500,000
- Implementation project (3-6 months): $100,000-$2,000,000
- Assessment/audit (2-4 weeks): $25,000-$150,000
- Workshop/facilitation (1-2 days): $5,000-$50,000

**Retainer / Subscription**
- Fixed monthly fee for ongoing access to services or reserved capacity
- Best for: ongoing advisory, fractional roles, maintenance/support
- Pros: predictable revenue, deepens relationship, lowers transaction cost
- Cons: utilization risk (over/under-usage), client may feel "paying for nothing" in slow months

**Typical benchmarks:**
- Fractional executive: $5,000-$25,000/month
- Ongoing advisory: $2,000-$15,000/month
- Managed services: $3,000-$50,000/month (depends on scope)

**Value-Based Pricing**
- Fee tied to the value or outcome delivered, not time spent
- Best for: high-impact engagements where ROI is measurable (cost reduction, revenue growth, M&A)
- Pros: aligns incentives, captures fair value, differentiates from competitors
- Cons: requires trust, hard to attribute outcomes, longer payment cycles

**Typical structures:**
- Fixed fee based on project value (e.g., 1-5% of identified savings)
- Base fee + success bonus (e.g., $100K base + 10% of incremental revenue above target)
- Gain-sharing: consultant receives percentage of documented savings for 1-3 years

**Success Fee / Contingency**
- Payment contingent on achieving specific outcomes
- Best for: M&A advisory, fundraising, sales/BD, litigation
- Pros: zero risk for client, strong alignment
- Cons: high risk for provider, selection bias, potential conflicts

**Typical benchmarks:**
- M&A advisory (Lehman formula variations): 1-5% of transaction value
- Fundraising: 2-8% of capital raised
- Sales consulting: 5-15% of new revenue generated
- Executive search: 20-33% of first-year compensation

### Professional Services Industry Norms
- Utilization target: 65-80% (billable hours / available hours)
- Realization rate: 85-95% (collected revenue / billed revenue)
- Revenue per consultant: $150,000-$500,000/year
- Profit margin: 15-25% for most firms; 30-40% for premium firms
- Effective rate escalation: 3-5% annually

## E-Commerce / Retail

### Pricing Models

**Cost-Plus Pricing**
- Price = Cost of Goods + Markup Percentage
- Best for: commodity products, private label, low-differentiation goods
- Pros: simple, guarantees minimum margin, easy to calculate
- Cons: ignores demand, ignores competition, may leave money on the table

**Typical markups by category:**
| Category | Typical Markup | Gross Margin |
|---|---|---|
| Grocery / food | 25-50% | 20-35% |
| Apparel | 100-300% (keystone to triple) | 50-75% |
| Electronics | 10-30% | 10-25% |
| Home goods / furniture | 100-200% | 50-65% |
| Beauty / cosmetics | 200-500% | 65-85% |
| Jewelry | 200-500% | 60-80% |
| Books | 40-60% | 30-40% |

**Keystone Pricing**
- Standard 2x markup (100% markup, 50% margin)
- Traditional retail rule of thumb
- Still common in apparel, home goods, specialty retail
- Adjust up for high-service, exclusive, or small-volume items; adjust down for commodity or high-competition

**Minimum Advertised Price (MAP)**
- Manufacturer sets minimum price that can be advertised publicly
- Retailers can sell below MAP in-store or in cart but cannot advertise below MAP
- Protects brand value and prevents race-to-bottom pricing
- Common in electronics, sporting goods, luxury goods

**Promotional Pricing**
- Temporary discounts to drive traffic, clear inventory, or acquire customers
- Types: percentage-off, BOGO, flash sale, seasonal clearance, bundle discount
- Best practices: limit frequency (avoid training customers to wait for sales), time-box promotions, track incremental lift vs. cannibalization

**Benchmarks:**
- Average e-commerce discount depth: 15-30%
- Promotional revenue as % of total: 20-40% (varies by category)
- Cart abandonment rate: 65-75%
- Promotional lift (incremental sales): 20-60% during promotion
- Post-promotion dip: 10-30% drop after promotion ends

### E-Commerce / Retail Industry Norms
- Average gross margin: 40-60% (varies significantly by category)
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC): $10-$50 (D2C), $1-$10 (marketplace)
- Return rate: 15-30% (apparel), 5-10% (electronics), 2-5% (grocery)
- Price elasticity: -1.5 to -3.0 for most consumer goods
- Shipping as pricing tool: free shipping threshold drives 15-25% AOV increase

## Manufacturing / B2B

### Pricing Models

**Volume Discount / Tiered Pricing**
- Price per unit decreases with quantity ordered
- Best for: standard products, repeat-purchase items, commoditized inputs
- Structure: price bands (e.g., 1-99 units at $10, 100-499 at $8, 500+ at $6)

**Typical discount structures:**
| Volume Tier | Discount from List |
|---|---|
| 1-99 units | List price |
| 100-499 | 10-15% |
| 500-999 | 15-25% |
| 1,000-4,999 | 20-30% |
| 5,000+ | 25-40% (negotiated) |

**Contract Pricing / Blanket Orders**
- Negotiated price for committed volume over a contract period (typically 1-3 years)
- Includes: volume commitment, delivery schedule, price escalation clause
- Pros: predictable revenue and supply for both parties
- Cons: locked-in pricing in volatile cost environments

**Index-Based / Cost-Plus Pricing**
- Price tied to a commodity index or cost input plus fixed margin
- Examples: steel price + 15% fabrication margin; oil index + $X processing fee
- Best for: products with significant raw material cost exposure
- Includes escalation/de-escalation clauses tied to published indices
- Common in: metals, chemicals, energy, agricultural products

**Value-Based / Engineered Pricing**
- Price based on value delivered (cost savings, performance improvement, yield increase)
- Requires deep understanding of customer economics
- Example: "Our coating extends equipment life by 3 years, saving $500K -- we price at $150K"

### Manufacturing / B2B Industry Norms
- Gross margin: 25-45% (varies by product complexity and differentiation)
- Typical contract length: 1-3 years
- Annual price escalator: CPI + 1-3% or input cost index
- Payment terms: Net 30-60 (standard), Net 90+ (large customers)
- Negotiation from list price: 15-30% average discount
- Rebate programs: 1-5% of annual purchases (volume incentive)

## Marketplace / Platform

### Pricing Models

**Take Rate (Commission)**
- Platform takes a percentage of each transaction
- Best for: transaction-heavy marketplaces (e-commerce, services, gig economy)

**Typical take rates by category:**
| Platform Type | Typical Take Rate |
|---|---|
| E-commerce marketplace | 8-15% |
| Food delivery | 15-30% |
| Ride-sharing | 20-30% |
| Freelance / services | 10-20% |
| Real estate | 2-6% |
| B2B marketplace | 3-10% |
| Digital content | 15-30% |
| App stores | 15-30% |

**Subscription / Membership**
- Fixed periodic fee for platform access, with or without transaction fees
- Examples: Amazon Prime, LinkedIn Premium, MLS access
- Best for: platforms where value is access (content, network, tools) rather than transactions
- Can be combined with take rate (subscription + reduced commission)

**Hybrid (Subscription + Transaction)**
- Monthly/annual fee plus per-transaction or per-listing fee
- Examples: Shopify (subscription + payment processing %), eBay (store subscription + final value fee)
- Best for: platforms where sellers need tools AND marketplace distribution

**Listing / Lead Fees**
- Charge per listing posted or per lead generated
- Examples: Zillow (lead gen), Indeed (sponsored listings), Craigslist (paid categories)
- Best for: platforms where the transaction happens offline

**Benchmarks:**
- Listing fee: $1-$50 per listing (varies by category value)
- Lead fee: $5-$500 per lead (based on lead value)
- Featured/promoted listings: 2-5x standard listing fee

### Marketplace Industry Norms
- Break-even GMV: $10M-$100M (depends on take rate and cost structure)
- Liquidity target: 30-60% of listings should result in transaction
- Seller retention: 60-80% year-over-year is healthy
- Buyer repeat rate: 40-60% within 12 months
- Average take rate across all marketplaces: 10-20%
- Platform gross margin: 60-80% (once at scale)

## Healthcare

### Pricing Models

**Fee-for-Service (FFS)**
- Provider bills per service, procedure, or visit rendered
- Based on CPT codes with reimbursement rates set by payers
- Pros: straightforward, rewards volume
- Cons: incentivizes overutilization, does not reward outcomes

**Benchmarks:**
- Medicare reimbursement is the baseline reference (commercial payers typically 120-250% of Medicare)
- Primary care visit: $100-$250 (commercial), $60-$120 (Medicare)
- Specialist visit: $150-$500 (commercial), $80-$200 (Medicare)
- Surgical procedure: $1,000-$50,000+ (varies enormously by complexity)

**Capitation**
- Fixed per-member-per-month (PMPM) payment for managing a patient population
- Provider assumes financial risk for the cost of care
- Best for: primary care, managed care, population health management
- Pros: predictable revenue, incentivizes prevention and efficiency
- Cons: risk of underpayment if population is sicker than average

**Benchmarks:**
- Primary care capitation: $15-$50 PMPM
- Full-risk capitation: $200-$800 PMPM (covers all services)
- Shared savings rate: 50-70% of savings below benchmark

**Bundled Payments**
- Single payment for an entire episode of care (e.g., hip replacement including surgery, rehab, follow-up)
- Provider manages cost within the bundle; keeps savings, absorbs overruns
- Best for: well-defined procedures with predictable costs
- Common bundles: joint replacement, cardiac surgery, maternity care

**Benchmarks:**
- Bundle discount vs. FFS: 5-15% savings
- Bundle warranty period: 30-90 days post-procedure
- Shared savings range: 40-60% of savings below target

**Value-Based Care**
- Reimbursement tied to quality metrics and patient outcomes
- Pay-for-performance bonuses/penalties based on quality scorecards
- Moving from volume to value across the industry
- Metrics: readmission rates, patient satisfaction, clinical outcomes, cost efficiency

**Benchmarks:**
- Quality bonus/penalty: 1-9% of total reimbursement
- Shared savings programs: 50-75% of savings, with downside risk caps of 5-10%
- Transition timeline: most systems still 60-70% FFS, targeting 50%+ value-based by 2030

### Healthcare Industry Norms
- Payer mix impact: commercial pays 150-300% of Medicare; Medicaid pays 70-90% of Medicare
- Charge-to-collection ratio: 30-40% (charges are inflated; collections are what matters)
- Bad debt / uncompensated care: 3-8% of net revenue
- Operating margin: 2-5% for hospitals (thin), 10-20% for physician practices, 15-30% for health tech
- Price transparency requirements: federal mandate for hospitals to publish prices (effective 2021+)
- Consolidation impact: merged health systems typically raise commercial prices 10-30%

## Cross-Industry Pricing Principles

### Universal Truths
1. **Price is a signal**: it communicates quality, positioning, and value before the customer experiences the product
2. **The best pricing aligns incentives**: customer pays more when they get more value
3. **Simplicity wins**: if the customer cannot understand the pricing in 30 seconds, it is too complex
4. **Test before you commit**: small-scale price tests prevent large-scale mistakes
5. **Grandfathering buys goodwill**: never surprise existing customers with sudden price increases

### Pricing Maturity Model

| Stage | Characteristics | Typical Approach | Improvement Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad hoc | No formal pricing process, gut-based | Cost-plus or competitor-matching | Implement basic pricing framework |
| Emerging | Some structure, limited data | Basic tiering, standard discounts | Add WTP research, competitive analysis |
| Structured | Formal process, regular reviews | Value-based, segmented pricing | Advanced analytics, dynamic pricing |
| Optimized | Data-driven, continuous optimization | Real-time pricing, ML-driven | Cultural integration, price innovation |
