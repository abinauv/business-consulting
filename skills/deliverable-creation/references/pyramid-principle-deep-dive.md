# The Pyramid Principle — Deep Dive

## Situation-Complication-Resolution (SCR) Worked Examples

### Example 1: Market Entry
- **Situation:** MedTech Corp is a $200M medical device company with 85% of revenue from the US market. The company has strong engineering capabilities and FDA regulatory expertise.
- **Complication:** US market growth has slowed to 3% annually as the market matures. Competitors are expanding internationally and gaining scale advantages. If MedTech doesn't diversify geographically, it risks losing competitive position within 3-5 years.
- **Resolution:** We recommend MedTech enter the European market through a targeted acquisition of a mid-size distributor ($30-50M revenue), leveraging its existing product portfolio and regulatory capabilities to achieve $100M in European revenue within 5 years.

### Example 2: Cost Reduction
- **Situation:** RetailCo operates 450 stores across 35 states with $2.1B in annual revenue. The company has invested heavily in digital transformation over the past 3 years.
- **Complication:** Operating margins have declined from 8.5% to 5.2% over the past two years as store-level labor costs and supply chain costs have increased faster than revenue growth. At current trajectory, the company will be EBITDA-negative within 18 months.
- **Resolution:** We recommend a three-phase cost transformation targeting $120M in annual savings: (1) immediate labor optimization through scheduling technology ($45M), (2) supply chain consolidation and renegotiation ($50M), and (3) portfolio rationalization of underperforming stores ($25M).

### Example 3: Digital Transformation
- **Situation:** RegionalBank is a top-20 US bank with $50B in assets. It serves 2.5M retail customers and 150K commercial clients, primarily through a 300-branch network.
- **Complication:** Digital-first competitors have captured 35% of new account openings in RegionalBank's markets. Customer surveys show NPS of 22 (vs. 55 for digital competitors). 60% of RegionalBank's technology budget is spent maintaining legacy systems, leaving little capacity for innovation.
- **Resolution:** We recommend a $150M, 3-year digital modernization program focused on three priorities: (1) migrate core banking to cloud ($60M), (2) launch a mobile-first customer experience ($50M), and (3) build a data analytics platform for personalized engagement ($40M). Expected ROI: 3.2x over 5 years.

## Deductive vs. Inductive Grouping

### Deductive Logic (Argument Chain)
Each statement follows logically from the previous:

**Parent:** "We should acquire CompanyX"
1. CompanyX has the distribution network we need to reach European customers (Major premise)
2. Building a distribution network organically would take 5+ years and cost 3x more than the acquisition (Minor premise)
3. Therefore, acquiring CompanyX is the fastest, most cost-effective path to European market entry (Conclusion)

Test: Does each step logically lead to the next? If you remove any step, does the argument break?

Best for: Making a persuasive case for a single recommendation

### Inductive Logic (Grouped Evidence)
Multiple observations point to a single conclusion:

**Parent:** "The market is becoming more competitive, requiring us to differentiate"
1. Three new competitors have entered the market in the last 18 months, each with $50M+ in funding
2. Average selling prices have declined 15% as competitors pursue market share
3. Customer switching rates have increased from 8% to 14% annually

Test: Do all items at the same level truly support the parent? Are they MECE? If you added one more item, would it change the conclusion?

Best for: Presenting evidence from multiple sources that converge on a single insight

## Structuring MECE Arguments

### Common MECE Frameworks

**By Time:** Short-term / Medium-term / Long-term (or Phase 1 / Phase 2 / Phase 3)

**By Geography:** North America / Europe / Asia-Pacific / Rest of World

**By Function:** Sales / Marketing / Operations / Finance / HR / IT

**By Customer Segment:** Enterprise / Mid-Market / SMB (or by industry vertical)

**By Value Chain Step:** Sourcing → Production → Distribution → Sales → Service

**By Impact Type:** Revenue growth / Cost reduction / Risk mitigation

**By Stakeholder:** Customers / Employees / Shareholders / Regulators

### MECE Test
For any grouping, ask:
1. **Mutually exclusive?** Can any item fit into more than one group? If yes, redefine boundaries.
2. **Collectively exhaustive?** Is anything important left out? If yes, add a group or use "Other."

### Common MECE Failures
- Overlapping categories: "Organic growth" and "New products" overlap (new products ARE organic growth)
- Missing categories: "Cost reduction" and "Revenue growth" miss "balance sheet optimization"
- Uneven granularity: "North America," "Europe," "Japan" — Japan is within Asia-Pacific, inconsistent level

### Chart Title Examples

| Before (Topic Title) | After (Action Title) |

| "Revenue by Segment" | "Enterprise segment drove 80% of revenue growth, offsetting SMB decline" |
| "Customer Satisfaction Survey Results" | "NPS improved from 22 to 38 after service redesign, now above industry median" |
| "Cost Structure Breakdown" | "Labor costs at 45% of revenue are 8pp above peer median, the largest savings opportunity" |
| "Competitive Market Share" | "We lost 3pp of market share to Competitor A, primarily in the mid-market segment" |
| "Employee Headcount Trend" | "Headcount grew 25% while revenue grew only 8%, widening the productivity gap" |
| "Technology Spend Overview" | "70% of IT budget goes to maintenance, leaving insufficient investment for growth initiatives" |

### Bullet Point Examples

**Before:** "We conducted interviews with 15 customers across 3 segments"
**After:** "Customer interviews revealed that speed of delivery (not price) is the primary purchase criterion — 12 of 15 customers ranked it first"

**Before:** "The European market is growing at 8% annually"
**After:** "Europe's 8% growth rate is 2x the US rate, making it the most attractive expansion market for the next 3 years"

## Common Pyramid Principle Mistakes

### 1. Burying the Lead
**Mistake:** Starting with methodology, context, or process before stating the answer.
**Fix:** Open every section, slide, and document with the conclusion. Methodology goes in the appendix.

### 2. False MECE Groupings
**Mistake:** Groups that seem MECE but actually overlap or have gaps.
**Fix:** Test rigorously. Ask: "Where does X fall?" for edge cases. If it could go in two groups, the grouping is wrong.

### 3. Circular Logic
**Mistake:** The supporting points restate the parent in different words rather than providing new evidence.
**Fix:** Each child must add new information. If you deleted the parent and read only the children, could you independently derive the parent?

### 4. Missing Vertical Logic
**Mistake:** The supporting points don't actually prove the parent statement.
**Fix:** For every parent-child relationship, ask: "Do these children PROVE the parent? Or do they just relate to it?"

### 5. Too Many Levels
**Mistake:** Creating 4-5 levels of hierarchy that lose the reader.
**Fix:** Most consulting arguments work with 2-3 levels. If you need more, the argument is too complex — simplify.

## Storyboarding a 20-Slide Deck

### Step-by-Step Process

**Step 1: Define the governing thought** (1 sentence)
This is the single most important message of the entire presentation. Everything else supports it.
Example: "MedTech should acquire EuroDistributor to enter the European market and achieve $100M in revenue within 5 years."

**Step 2: Identify 3-4 supporting pillars** (MECE)
These become your major sections:
1. The European market opportunity is large and growing
2. Acquisition is superior to organic entry
3. EuroDistributor is the best acquisition target
4. The financial returns justify the investment

**Step 3: Write action titles for every slide**

| # | Section | Action Title |
|---|---------|--------------|
| 1 | Cover | Strategy for European Market Entry |
| 2 | Agenda | Agenda |
| 3 | Exec Summary | We recommend acquiring EuroDistributor for $45M to enter Europe and achieve $100M revenue in 5 years |
| 4 | Market | European medical device market is $12B and growing at 8% annually, 2x the US growth rate |
| 5 | Market | Three segments offer the strongest fit: orthopedics, cardiology, and diagnostics |
| 6 | Market | Regulatory trends favor established players with FDA-approved products |
| 7 | Entry Options | Three entry options exist: organic, partnership, and acquisition |
| 8 | Entry Options | Organic entry requires 5+ years and $80M investment with high execution risk |
| 9 | Entry Options | Acquisition offers fastest time-to-revenue with lowest total cost of entry |
| 10 | Target | EuroDistributor is the strongest acquisition target based on strategic fit, financial profile, and cultural alignment |
| 11 | Target | EuroDistributor's distribution network covers 12 countries with 800+ hospital relationships |
| 12 | Target | EuroDistributor's financial profile is attractive: $40M revenue, 15% EBITDA margin, growing at 12% |
| 13 | Financials | Acquisition at $45M (6x EBITDA) with $20M revenue synergies yields 3.5x ROI over 5 years |
| 14 | Financials | Revenue synergies come from cross-selling MedTech's product portfolio through EuroDistributor's network |
| 15 | Financials | Cost synergies of $5M annually from procurement consolidation and back-office integration |
| 16 | Financials | Sensitivity analysis shows positive NPV in all scenarios except the downside case |
| 17 | Risks | Three key risks exist, each with a defined mitigation plan |
| 18 | Implementation | 18-month integration plan in three phases: close, stabilize, and grow |
| 19 | Next Steps | Three decisions needed from the board: approve LOI, authorize due diligence budget, appoint integration lead |
| 20 | Appendix | Appendix divider |

**Step 4: Read titles aloud and test the flow**
- Does each slide logically follow the previous?
- Can a partner understand the full story from titles alone?
- Are there any logical gaps?

**Step 5: Build content for each slide**
Only now start creating charts, tables, and supporting text.
