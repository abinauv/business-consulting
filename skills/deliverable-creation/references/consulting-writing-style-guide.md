# Consulting Writing Style Guide

## Voice and Tone

**Authoritative but not arrogant:** Present findings with confidence. Use "We recommend" not "We think." But acknowledge uncertainty: "Based on available data" not "Definitively."

**Direct but not blunt:** Lead with conclusions. But provide enough context for the reader to follow your logic.

**Precise but not pedantic:** Quantify everything possible. But don't add false precision ("approximately $47,283,291" — just say "$47M").

**Client-centric:** Frame everything in terms of impact on the client. Not "The market is growing" but "The growing market creates a $50M opportunity for [Client]."

## Before and After Examples

### 1. Burying the Lead
**Before:** "In order to better understand the competitive dynamics of the European medical device market, we conducted an analysis of 15 competitors across 8 dimensions, including revenue growth, margin profile, geographic presence, and product portfolio breadth. The results of this analysis suggest that the market is becoming more competitive."
**After:** "The European medical device market is intensifying — three new entrants in the last 18 months have compressed margins by 5pp. [Client] must differentiate on service or risk further share loss."

### 2. Vague Quantification
**Before:** "Revenue has grown significantly over the past few years."
**After:** "Revenue grew 34% over three years ($120M in FY22 to $161M in FY25), driven primarily by enterprise segment expansion."

### 3. Passive Voice
**Before:** "A decision to enter the European market was made by the board based on the recommendation that was presented."
**After:** "The board approved European market entry based on our recommendation."

### 4. Unnecessary Preamble
**Before:** "It is important to note that when we look at the data from a holistic perspective, taking into account all of the various factors at play, the conclusion that emerges is that costs are too high."
**After:** "Operating costs are 15% above peer median, driven by SGA inefficiency."

### 5. Jargon Without Context
**Before:** "We need to leverage our core competencies to drive synergistic value creation across the portfolio."
**After:** "We should use our engineering expertise (our strongest capability) to develop products for two adjacent markets, adding an estimated $30M in revenue."

### 6. List Without Parallel Structure
**Before:** "Key priorities: improve customer retention, we need to reduce costs, hiring better talent, and the technology platform should be upgraded."
**After:** "Key priorities: (1) improve customer retention from 85% to 92%, (2) reduce operating costs by $15M, (3) hire 20 senior engineers, and (4) upgrade the technology platform to cloud-native."

### 7. Missing "So What"
**Before:** "45% of customers are in the SMB segment."
**After:** "45% of customers are in the SMB segment, but they contribute only 15% of revenue — suggesting an opportunity to either monetize SMB more effectively or reallocate resources to the higher-value enterprise segment."

### 8. Wishy-Washy Recommendation
**Before:** "There are several options the company could potentially consider, and each has its own merits and drawbacks that should be carefully weighed."
**After:** "We recommend Option B (European acquisition) over organic entry or partnership. Option B delivers the highest risk-adjusted NPV ($45M) and fastest time-to-revenue (18 months vs. 4+ years)."

### 9. Run-On Sentence
**Before:** "The company has been experiencing declining margins over the past several quarters which is primarily driven by increasing raw material costs as well as labor cost inflation in key markets while at the same time facing competitive pricing pressure from new entrants who are willing to sacrifice profitability for market share."
**After:** "Margins have declined for four consecutive quarters, driven by three factors: (1) raw material costs up 12%, (2) labor inflation of 8% in key markets, and (3) pricing pressure from new entrants sacrificing profit for share."

### 10. Over-Hedging
**Before:** "It would appear that there may be a possible opportunity to potentially reduce costs in certain areas, although further analysis might be needed to validate these preliminary findings."
**After:** "We identified $15M in cost reduction opportunities across three areas. Two require further validation (estimated 2 weeks of additional analysis)."

## Bullet Point Construction

### The Bolded Lead-In Pattern
Every bullet point in a consulting document follows this structure:

**[Bolded lead-in phrase]:** Supporting detail with evidence or context.

Examples:
- **Enterprise segment drove growth:** Revenue from enterprise customers grew 28% ($45M to $58M), accounting for 85% of total growth.
- **SMB churn accelerated:** Monthly churn rate increased from 2.1% to 3.4%, driven by competitive pricing from new market entrants.
- **Operational efficiency declined:** Revenue per employee fell 12% as headcount grew faster than revenue, indicating a need for automation investment.

### Parallel Structure Rules
All bullets in a list must:
- Start with the same part of speech (all verbs, all nouns, or all adjectives)
- Follow the same grammatical pattern
- Be at a similar level of specificity

**Wrong:** "Reduce costs, customer satisfaction needs to improve, and hiring"
**Right:** "Reduce operating costs by $15M, improve customer NPS from 22 to 40, hire 20 senior engineers"

## Number Formatting Conventions

| Element | Convention | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Currency (large) | $XXM or $X.XB, no decimals unless needed | $45M, $2.3B |
| Currency (small) | $X,XXX with commas | $12,500 |
| Percentages | XX% with no decimal for whole numbers, X.X% when precision matters | 12%, 3.5% |
| Growth rates | Include sign: +12% or -3% | +12% YoY |
| Basis points | Use "pp" (percentage points) for changes in percentages | "Margin improved 3pp" |
| Ratios | Use X:Y format | LTV:CAC of 3.5:1 |
| Multiples | Use X× format | Valued at 8× EBITDA |
| Large numbers | Use K, M, B suffixes | 150K customers, $45M revenue |
| Dates | MMM YYYY or FY20XX | Jan 2025, FY2025 |
| Fiscal years | FY20XX (clarify if non-calendar) | FY2025 (ends Jun 2025) |
| Ranges | Use en-dash: X–Y | $40M–$60M, 12%–18% |
| Decimals | One decimal max in most cases | 12.3%, $45.2M |

## Capitalization and Acronym Rules

- **First use:** Spell out the full term followed by the acronym in parentheses: "Total Addressable Market (TAM)"
- **Subsequent uses:** Use the acronym only: "The TAM is $12B"
- **Common acronyms that don't need spelling out:** CEO, CFO, ROI, P&L, IPO, M&A, IT, HR, KPI (in business contexts)
- **Capitalize:** Proper nouns, company names, product names, framework names (Porter's Five Forces, SWOT)
- **Don't capitalize:** Generic business terms (market, industry, segment, revenue) unless at the start of a sentence

## Consulting Jargon Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Addressable market** | The portion of total market a company can realistically target |
| **Accretive** | An acquisition or action that increases earnings per share or returns |
| **ARPU** | Average Revenue Per User — recurring revenue per customer |
| **Basis points (bps)** | 1/100th of a percentage point. 100 bps = 1% |
| **Blue ocean** | An uncontested market space with no direct competition |
| **Burn rate** | Monthly cash expenditure (startups) |
| **CAGR** | Compound Annual Growth Rate over a multi-year period |
| **Cohort** | A group of customers or items sharing a common characteristic (e.g., sign-up month) |
| **Comps** | Comparable company analysis — valuation by peer multiples |
| **Core competency** | A unique capability that provides competitive advantage across multiple products/markets |
| **D2C** | Direct-to-consumer — bypassing traditional distribution channels |
| **Dilutive** | An action that decreases earnings per share or returns |
| **EBITDA** | Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization |
| **Go-to-market (GTM)** | The strategy for reaching and selling to target customers |
| **Greenfield** | Building something new from scratch (vs. acquiring or partnering) |
| **Headwinds** | Factors working against the business or market |
| **Hockey stick** | A growth projection showing slow start followed by rapid acceleration |
| **LTV** | Lifetime Value — total revenue expected from a customer over their lifespan |
| **Moat** | A sustainable competitive advantage that protects against competition |
| **Net revenue retention (NRR)** | Revenue from existing customers including expansion, contraction, and churn |
| **Operating leverage** | The degree to which profits increase with revenue growth (high fixed costs = high leverage) |
| **PLG** | Product-Led Growth — product itself drives acquisition and expansion |
| **Run rate** | Current period revenue annualized (e.g., Q1 revenue × 4) |
| **Runway** | Months of cash remaining at current burn rate |
| **Synergies** | Value created by combining two entities (cost synergies = savings; revenue synergies = growth) |
| **TAM/SAM/SOM** | Total/Serviceable/Obtainable market sizes (nested) |
| **Tailwinds** | Factors working in favor of the business or market |
| **Unit economics** | Per-customer profitability (CAC, LTV, payback period) |
| **Value chain** | The full sequence of activities from raw materials to end customer |
| **White space** | An unserved market opportunity or gap in competitive offerings |

## Words and Phrases to Avoid

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|-------|------------|
| "Very" / "Really" | Delete or quantify: "very large" → "$2B" |
| "Significant" (without number) | Quantify: "significant growth" → "34% growth" |
| "Going forward" | "Over the next 12 months" or just delete |
| "At this point in time" | "Currently" or "Now" |
| "In order to" | "To" |
| "It should be noted that" | Delete — just state the note |
| "As mentioned previously" | Delete or add a page reference |
| "Utilize" | "Use" |
| "Leverage" (as verb) | "Use" or "Apply" |
| "Synergize" | Don't. Just explain the specific benefit. |
| "Holistic" | "Comprehensive" or specify what you mean |
| "Paradigm shift" | Describe the specific change |
| "Low-hanging fruit" | "Quick wins" or name the specific opportunities |
| "Move the needle" | Quantify: "increase revenue by $5M" |
| "Deep dive" (as verb) | "Analyze" or "Investigate" |

## Executive Summary — Before and After

### Before (Weak)
"We have conducted an extensive analysis of the client's market position, competitive environment, and financial performance. Our findings suggest that there are several areas where improvement may be possible. We believe that with the right strategy, the company could potentially see improved performance going forward. We recommend that the leadership team consider the options presented in this report."

### After (Strong)
"**[Client] should pursue a three-phase transformation to restore margins from 5% to 12% within 24 months.** Our analysis identifies $120M in annual savings opportunities:

- **Labor optimization ($45M):** Scheduling technology and workforce restructuring will reduce labor costs by 15%, bringing them in line with peer median
- **Supply chain consolidation ($50M):** Consolidating from 340 to 120 suppliers and renegotiating key contracts will reduce COGS by 8%
- **Portfolio rationalization ($25M):** Closing 45 underperforming stores (bottom 10% by contribution margin) will eliminate $25M in annual losses

**The investment required is $35M over 18 months, yielding a 3.4× ROI.** Phase 1 (quick wins) begins immediately and delivers $30M in the first 6 months."
