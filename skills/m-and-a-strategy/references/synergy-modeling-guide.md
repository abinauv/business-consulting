# Synergy Modeling Guide

## Revenue Synergy Categories

| Category | Description | Typical Realization | Confidence Level | Estimation Method |
|----------|-----------|:-------------------:|:----------------:|------------------|
| **Cross-sell** | Sell acquirer's products to target's customers (and vice versa) | 20–40% realized by Year 2 | Medium | Customer overlap analysis, ASP × conversion rate |
| **Up-sell** | Move combined customer base to higher-value offerings | 30–50% by Year 2 | Medium | Pricing analysis, tier migration modeling |
| **Geographic expansion** | Use target's distribution in new markets | 15–30% by Year 3 | Low-Medium | Market sizing × realistic share capture |
| **New products** | Combine capabilities to create new offerings | 10–20% by Year 3 | Low | Market sizing, comparable product launches |
| **Pricing power** | Increased market share enables pricing improvement | 20–40% by Year 2 | Medium | Elasticity analysis, competitive dynamics |
| **Customer retention** | Combined offering reduces churn | Immediate | Medium-High | Churn reduction × revenue at risk |

**Key rule:** Revenue synergies are harder to capture and take longer than cost synergies. Discount revenue synergies by 50% for conservative modeling.

## Cost Synergy Categories

| Category | Description | Typical Range (% of combined cost base) | Realization Timeline | Confidence |
|----------|-----------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Headcount** | Eliminate duplicate roles (C-suite, finance, HR, IT, admin) | 10–25% of overlapping G&A | Year 1–2 | High |
| **Procurement** | Combined purchasing power, contract renegotiation | 3–8% of combined procurement spend | Year 1–2 | Medium-High |
| **Real estate** | Consolidate offices, warehouses, branches | 10–30% of combined facility costs | Year 2–3 | Medium |
| **IT/Technology** | Consolidate systems, licenses, infrastructure | 10–20% of combined IT spend | Year 2–3 | Medium |
| **Shared services** | Centralize finance, HR, legal, procurement | 15–25% of centralized function costs | Year 2–3 | Medium |
| **Sales & marketing** | Eliminate redundant campaigns, combine sales teams | 5–15% of combined S&M spend | Year 1–2 | Medium |

### Typical Cost Synergy Ranges by Deal Type

| Deal Type | Total Synergies (% of smaller entity's cost base) |
|-----------|:--:|
| Horizontal (same industry, similar size) | 15–25% |
| Horizontal (acquirer much larger) | 20–35% |

| Technology/platform | 10–20% |

## Capital Synergy Categories

| Category | Description | Typical Impact |
|----------|-----------|:---:|
| **Working capital** | Optimize combined inventory, receivables, payables | 5–15% improvement in cash conversion cycle |
| **Capex optimization** | Eliminate duplicate investments, share capacity | 5–10% of combined capex |
| **Tax benefits** | NOL utilization, transfer pricing, jurisdictional optimization | Highly deal-specific |
| **Financing** | Better credit terms, lower cost of capital from scale | 25–75 bps improvement |

## Synergy Quantification Methodology

### Bottom-Up Build Process

1. **List all synergy opportunities** — Brainstorm by category (revenue, cost, capital)
2. **Estimate gross potential** — What's the maximum if fully realized?
3. **Apply probability discount** — Confidence-weight each opportunity
4. **Phase over time** — When will each synergy be captured? (Year 1, 2, 3)
5. **Estimate one-time costs** — What does it cost to achieve each synergy? (severance, system migration, lease break fees)
6. **Calculate net present value** — Discount future synergies to present value

### Confidence Weighting Framework

| Confidence Level | Discount Factor | Criteria | Examples |
|:----------------:|:-:|-----------|---------|
| **High (80–90%)** | 0.85 | Clear line of sight, contractual, or historically proven | Headcount reduction (identified roles), contract renegotiation (known terms) |
| **Medium (50–70%)** | 0.60 | Reasonable basis, requires execution, some uncertainty | Cross-sell (estimated), facility consolidation (planned), IT consolidation |
| **Low (20–40%)** | 0.30 | Aspirational, depends on market conditions or customer response | New product revenue, pricing power, geographic expansion |

## One-Time Costs to Achieve Synergies

Don't forget integration costs:

| Cost Category | Typical Range | Notes |
|-------------|:---:|---|
| Severance and retention | $50K–$200K per affected employee | Higher for senior/executive roles |
| System migration/integration | $2M–$20M+ | Depends on complexity; ERP consolidation is most expensive |
| Facility consolidation | $1M–$10M | Lease breaks, moving costs, renovation |
| Rebranding | $0.5M–$5M | Signage, digital, collateral, legal |
| Professional fees (legal, tax, advisory) | $2M–$10M | Scales with deal size |
| Change management and communication | $0.5M–$2M | Often underbudgeted |
| Retention bonuses for key talent | $1M–$10M+ | Critical for talent-driven acquisitions |

**Rule of thumb:** One-time costs typically equal 50–100% of Year 1 synergies. Synergies usually take 2–3 years to fully realize, so NPV of synergies should meaningfully exceed one-time costs.

## Worked Example: $500M Acquisition Synergy Model

**Context:** $2B revenue industrial company acquiring a $500M competitor in the same market.

### Deal Overview
- Acquirer revenue: $2.0B, EBITDA: $300M (15% margin)
- Target revenue: $500M, EBITDA: $60M (12% margin)
- Purchase price: $500M (8.3x EBITDA)
- Combined revenue: $2.5B

### Synergy Build

| # | Category | Opportunity | Detail | Gross | Confidence | Weighted | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | OTC |
|---|----------|-----------|--------|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 1 | Cost | Executive overlap | Eliminate CFO, CHRO, CMO, CLO + their teams (18 people) | $4.5M | High (90%) | $4.1M | $3.5M | $4.1M | $4.1M | $3.0M |
| 2 | Cost | Shared services | Centralize AP/AR, payroll, IT helpdesk (25 people) | $3.2M | High (80%) | $2.6M | $0.5M | $2.0M | $2.6M | $1.5M |
| 3 | Cost | Procurement | Combined purchasing: raw materials, packaging, logistics | $8.0M | Medium (65%) | $5.2M | $1.5M | $3.5M | $5.2M | $0.5M |
| 4 | Cost | Facility consolidation | Close 2 of 7 distribution centers | $3.5M | Medium (60%) | $2.1M | $0M | $1.0M | $2.1M | $4.0M |
| 5 | Cost | IT consolidation | Migrate to single ERP, consolidate licenses | $2.8M | Medium (55%) | $1.5M | $0M | $0.5M | $1.5M | $6.0M |
| 6 | Cost | S&M overlap | Consolidate marketing teams, eliminate duplicate campaigns | $2.0M | Medium (70%) | $1.4M | $0.7M | $1.4M | $1.4M | $0.5M |
| 7 | Revenue | Cross-sell | Sell acquirer's premium products through target's distribution | $6.0M | Low (35%) | $2.1M | $0M | $0.7M | $2.1M | $0.8M |
| 8 | Revenue | Pricing | Reduced competition enables 1–2% price increase | $5.0M | Low (30%) | $1.5M | $0M | $0.5M | $1.5M | $0M |
| | | **TOTAL** | | **$35.0M** | | **$20.5M** | **$6.2M** | **$13.7M** | **$20.5M** | **$16.3M** |

### Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|:-----:|
| Purchase price | $500M |
| Total run-rate synergies (gross) | $35.0M |
| Total run-rate synergies (confidence-weighted) | $20.5M |
| One-time costs to achieve | $16.3M |
| Net Year 1 benefit | -$10.1M (investment year) |
| Net Year 2 benefit | $13.7M |
| Run-rate benefit (Year 3+) | $20.5M |
| Effective purchase multiple (post-synergies) | 6.2x ($500M ÷ ($60M + $20.5M)) |
| % of purchase price covered by synergies (PV, 3 years) | ~9% |

**Key insight:** The deal effectively reduces the purchase multiple from 8.3x to 6.2x EBITDA when synergies are fully realized. Cost synergies ($16.9M weighted) are highly actionable; revenue synergies ($3.6M weighted) are upside optionality.
