# Industry Valuation Multiples Reference

This reference provides approximate valuation multiple ranges across major industry sectors. These figures represent typical ranges observed in public markets and M&A transactions. Actual multiples vary significantly based on company-specific factors including growth rate, profitability, competitive position, and market conditions at the time of valuation. Use these as directional benchmarks rather than precise inputs.

---

## SaaS / Software

**EV/Revenue Multiples by Growth Rate:**

| Growth Tier | EV/Revenue Range | EV/ARR Range | Typical Profile |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Below 20% growth | 3x - 6x | 3x - 7x | Mature, profitable SaaS |
| 20% - 40% growth | 6x - 12x | 7x - 14x | Scaling, improving margins |
| Above 40% growth | 10x - 20x+ | 12x - 25x+ | Hypergrowth, market leaders |

**Rule of 40 Correlation:**
The Rule of 40 (revenue growth % + EBITDA margin % should exceed 40%) is strongly correlated with valuation premiums. Companies scoring above 40 on this metric typically trade at a 30-50% premium to peers. Companies above 60 can command top-decile multiples.

**Key Premium Drivers:** Net revenue retention above 120%, gross margins above 75%, large TAM with low penetration, platform or ecosystem characteristics, strong free cash flow conversion.

**Key Discount Drivers:** High churn (gross retention below 85%), customer concentration (top 10 customers represent more than 30% of ARR), low switching costs, heavy reliance on professional services revenue.

## Fintech

**EV/Revenue:** 5x - 15x (wide range depending on business model)

| Sub-Sector | Typical EV/Revenue | Key Metric |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------|
| Payments / Processing | 5x - 10x | Total Payment Volume (TPV), take rate |
| Lending Platforms | 3x - 8x | Loan origination volume, net interest margin |
| Insurance Tech | 4x - 12x | Gross Written Premium, loss ratio |
| Wealth / Investment | 6x - 15x | Assets Under Management (AUM), net flows |
| Banking-as-a-Service | 8x - 18x | API call volume, number of embedded partners |

**Premium Drivers:** Regulatory moats (banking licenses, compliance infrastructure), network effects in payments, high revenue per account, low marginal cost of serving additional customers.

**Discount Drivers:** Regulatory risk, credit risk exposure on balance sheet, reliance on interchange fees subject to regulatory caps, high customer acquisition costs in competitive markets.

## Healthcare / Biotech

**Established Healthcare Companies:**

| Sub-Sector | EV/Revenue | EV/EBITDA |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Healthcare Services | 1x - 3x | 8x - 14x |
| Medical Devices | 3x - 7x | 15x - 25x |
| Health IT / Digital Health | 5x - 12x | 20x - 35x |
| Managed Care / Insurance | 0.5x - 1.5x | 10x - 16x |

**Biotech / Pharmaceuticals (Clinical Stage):**
Pre-revenue biotech is valued using risk-adjusted NPV of the drug pipeline rather than revenue multiples. Key factors include phase of clinical trials (Phase I vs. Phase III success probabilities differ dramatically), addressable patient population, pricing power, and patent life remaining.

- Phase I asset: typically valued at 5-15% of peak revenue potential
- Phase II asset: typically valued at 15-35% of peak revenue potential
- Phase III asset: typically valued at 40-70% of peak revenue potential
- Approved drug: closer to full NPV of projected revenue stream

**Premium Drivers:** Platform technology applicable across therapeutic areas, orphan drug designation, first-in-class mechanism, strong IP portfolio with long patent runway.

## Consumer / Retail

| Sub-Sector | EV/EBITDA | P/E | EV/Revenue |
|-----------|----------|-----|-----------|
| Luxury / Premium Brands | 15x - 25x | 25x - 40x | 3x - 6x |
| Fast-Moving Consumer Goods | 10x - 16x | 18x - 28x | 2x - 4x |
| Discount / Value Retail | 7x - 11x | 12x - 20x | 0.3x - 1x |
| DTC / E-Commerce | 8x - 15x | 15x - 30x | 1x - 4x |
| Restaurants / QSR | 10x - 18x | 18x - 30x | 2x - 5x |
| Grocery | 6x - 9x | 12x - 18x | 0.2x - 0.5x |

**Premium Drivers:** Brand strength and pricing power, high same-store sales growth, asset-light franchise model, loyal customer base with high repeat rates, strong omnichannel presence.

**Discount Drivers:** Declining foot traffic, fashion or trend risk, thin margins with no clear path to improvement, high inventory obsolescence risk, lack of differentiation.

## Energy

| Sub-Sector | EV/EBITDA | Alternative Metrics |
|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| Integrated Oil & Gas | 4x - 7x | EV per barrel of proved reserves |
| E&P (Exploration & Production) | 3x - 6x | EV per flowing BOE, EV per acre |
| Midstream (Pipelines) | 8x - 12x | EV/Distributable Cash Flow |
| Renewables / Clean Energy | 10x - 20x | EV per MW installed capacity |
| Utilities | 8x - 12x | Regulated asset base (RAB) multiple |
| Energy Services / OFS | 4x - 8x | Backlog, rig count sensitivity |

**Premium Drivers:** Long reserve life, low-cost production assets, contracted cash flows (take-or-pay), favorable regulatory environment for renewables, ESG positioning attracting capital.

**Discount Drivers:** High decline rates requiring constant reinvestment, commodity price volatility, regulatory and environmental risk, stranded asset risk for fossil fuels, geopolitical exposure.

## Real Estate

| Sub-Sector | Cap Rate Range | Price/FFO | Key Driver |
|-----------|---------------|----------|-----------|
| Industrial / Logistics | 4% - 6% | 20x - 30x | E-commerce demand, location |
| Multifamily Residential | 4% - 6% | 18x - 28x | Rent growth, occupancy |
| Office | 6% - 9% | 10x - 16x | Return-to-office trends, class A vs. B |
| Retail | 5% - 8% | 12x - 18x | Tenant quality, foot traffic |
| Data Centers | 4% - 6% | 22x - 35x | Power capacity, cloud demand |
| Self-Storage | 5% - 7% | 18x - 25x | Revenue per sq ft, occupancy |

Cap rate = Net Operating Income / Property Value. Lower cap rates imply higher valuations and perceived lower risk.

**Premium Drivers:** Prime locations in supply-constrained markets, long-term leases with credit tenants, embedded rent escalators, development pipeline in high-demand sectors.

**Discount Drivers:** Exposure to work-from-home trends (office), tenant concentration, deferred maintenance or capital needs, markets with oversupply.

## Financial Services

| Sub-Sector | P/E | P/BV (Book Value) | Key Driver |
|-----------|-----|-------|-----------|
| Large Banks | 8x - 14x | 0.8x - 1.5x | ROE, net interest margin |
| Regional Banks | 8x - 12x | 0.8x - 1.3x | Deposit franchise, credit quality |
| Insurance (Life) | 8x - 12x | 0.7x - 1.2x | Embedded value, investment returns |
| Insurance (P&C) | 10x - 15x | 1.0x - 2.0x | Combined ratio, reserve adequacy |
| Asset Management | 10x - 18x | 2x - 5x | AUM, fee rates, flow trends |
| Exchanges & Data | 18x - 30x | 5x - 10x | Trading volume, data revenue |

**ROE as the Core Driver:** For banks and insurance companies, the Price-to-Book multiple is closely tied to return on equity. Companies generating ROE above their cost of equity trade above 1x book value. Every 2-3 percentage points of ROE above cost of equity typically adds 0.2x-0.3x to the P/BV multiple.

**Premium Drivers:** Consistent ROE above 15%, diversified fee income, strong deposit franchise with low cost of funds, clean credit quality through cycles, scalable asset-light platforms.

**Discount Drivers:** ROE below cost of equity, high credit losses, regulatory capital constraints, interest rate sensitivity, operational risk from legacy technology.

## Important Caveats

1. **Market conditions matter enormously.** Multiples compress in risk-off environments and expand in risk-on environments. These ranges represent mid-cycle norms but actual multiples at any given time may be 20-40% above or below these ranges.

2. **Size premium exists.** Larger companies within each sector typically command higher multiples due to lower perceived risk, better liquidity, and broader investor coverage.

3. **Geographic adjustments.** US-listed companies generally trade at premium multiples compared to European or Asian peers in the same sector, partly due to market depth and growth expectations.

4. **Private company discount.** Private companies are typically valued at a 15-30% discount to public peers due to illiquidity, less transparency, and higher key-person risk.

5. **Always use multiple methods.** No single multiple tells the full story. Cross-reference EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, and P/E to build a holistic picture and identify inconsistencies.
